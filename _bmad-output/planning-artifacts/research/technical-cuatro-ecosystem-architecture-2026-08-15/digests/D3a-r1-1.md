What I can say without those numbers: on 2 vCPU / 8 GB, with 12 apps competing for RAM, a proxy delta of even 150 MB is ~2% of total memory and is **not the binding constraint**: the Next.js SSR and Elixir BEAM processes are. Choosing the proxy on measured RSS is optimising the wrong variable, and the evidence for doing so is currently worthless.

## Subdomain vs path routing: evidenced consequences

The only high-quality retrospective found (Duke, Buttondown, 2026-02-18, 8 years in) lands on **subdomains**, and its strongest arguments transfer directly to a 12-app suite on one apex:

- **Browser origin isolation is the load-bearing one.** Subdomains are separate origins: cookies, `localStorage`, and XSS blast radius are sandboxed per app. Subpaths share one origin, a compromise in one of twelve apps reaches the other eleven's client-side state. For a suite mixing Next.js, Phoenix, Fastify, Go, and Python of varying maturity, that is a real and asymmetric risk. [V]
- **SEO blast radius is shared under subpaths.** Duke's exact case: one abusive user on a subpath damages the whole domain's reputation; under subdomains the damage is isolated. [V]
- **The counter-argument he grants is SSO/session sharing**: subpaths mean one origin, so cookies and OAuth redirect URIs are configured once. Under subdomains you need `Domain=.apex.tld` cookies (which reintroduces some coupling) or a wildcard redirect URI. Search results independently framed this same trade-off. [V, medium]
- **basePath burden is real but not quantified this run.** The Next.js discussion thread (vercel/next.js#48576) and SvelteKit issue #8335 confirm framework-level friction exists around combining basePath with host routing, but I did not read them and cannot state what breaks in current versions. [U]
- **Certificate management under subdomains is cheap in this specific environment**: DNS on Cloudflare means one DNS-01 wildcard `*.apex.tld` covers all twelve apps, one cert, one renewal, one failure surface, and no per-app HTTP-01 reachability requirement. That neutralises the classic "subdomains mean more certs" objection. [V, by composition of LE + Cloudflare facts]

**Verdict on topology: subdomains.** The evidence points one way, the counter-argument (SSO) is solvable with domain-scoped cookies, and the usual cost (certs) is eliminated by the Cloudflare DNS-01 wildcard. Several subdomains are already live, so this is also the status-quo-preserving choice.

## Cloudflare proxy mode: what the amendment changes

- Cloudflare's own docs give **no per-plan WebSocket idle timeout**, only "implement a keepalive". The "100 seconds on Free/Pro" figure circulating widely is not in the primary. [V]
- Persistent-WebSocket disconnects behind the Cloudflare proxy are a **recurring reported failure class** across community threads and cloudflared issues. [low-medium confidence]
- **Practical read for LiveView:** orange-cloud is probably survivable because Phoenix heartbeats, but this is the single highest-risk unverified assumption in this digest. If certainty matters more than Cloudflare's WAF/caching, grey-cloud (DNS-only) the LiveView subdomain and let the origin proxy terminate TLS, that removes the entire question. DNS-01 wildcard issuance works identically either way, which is another point for DNS-01 over HTTP-01.
- **Flexible vs Full-Strict:** not evidenced this run. The redirect-loop failure under Flexible mode is well-known to me but I retrieved nothing on it, see below.

## Leads worth chasing

1. `https://blog.tjll.net/reverse-proxy-hot-dog-eating-contest-caddy-vs-nginx/`: the one methodologically serious independent benchmark surfaced. Read it, and find or run an equivalent including Traefik. Better still: **measure it yourself on the actual VPS**; one afternoon beats every blog post.
2. Verify `https://caddyserver.com/docs/modules/dns.providers.cloudflare` directly: is the custom build still mandatory in 2.11.4, and is there now an official pre-built variant? This is the swing factor between Caddy and Traefik for this operator.
3. Confirm Traefik v3 ships the Cloudflare DNS-01 provider in the standard `traefik:v3` Docker image (I believe yes; unverified). If true, Traefik wins the TLS-config-burden comparison outright in this environment.
4. Read `https://developers.cloudflare.com/ssl/origin-configuration/ssl-modes/` for Flexible/Full/Full-Strict and the documented redirect loop; and evaluate Cloudflare Origin Certificates (15-year lifetime, valid only behind the proxy) as an alternative to Let's Encrypt for proxied origins: this would sidestep the 45-day renewal treadmill entirely for proxied subdomains.
5. Verify Phoenix's default `heartbeatIntervalMs` in current phoenix_live_view docs. One line of evidence closes the biggest open risk here.
6. Release-cadence audit of the PaaS layer (Kamal, Dokploy, Coolify, Dokku, CapRover) against GitHub commit/release history, **not** comparison blogs. Search surfaced a claim that Coolify v4.0.0 went stable in April 2026 after ~2 years of beta with a v5 rewrite underway: a v5 rewrite mid-flight is a stability signal worth checking before adopting. Note that most tools in that space bundle their own proxy (Coolify and Dokploy both use Traefik), which would make the proxy decision downstream of the PaaS decision rather than upstream: that ordering question belongs to whoever owns D3b.
7. Screened but not eliminated with evidence: **Nginx Proxy Manager** (GUI over nginx; check maintenance cadence and whether DNS-01 wildcard is first-class), **HAProxy** (no built-in ACME historically; strong on TCP/L4 but config burden is high for 12 HTTP apps), **Pingora** (a Rust *library*, not a deployable proxy, the honest cut is that a solo operator would be writing a proxy, not configuring one; River, the Pingora-based server, needs a maturity check).
