# The font contract

The written record of `contracts/fonts.css` and the three faces beside it: what the type contract
publishes, where the licences sit and why, the subsetting decisions with the measured cost that
forced them, how the metric overrides are derived, the budget figures with their method, how the
faces and the stylesheet are regenerated, the two gates with their probe demonstrations, the stated
limits, and the work this file hands the Operator.

Written during Story 1-12 on **2026-08-25** (ISO 8601 UTC), against baseline commit `7545cd6`.

This file is a record, not Registry data, and nothing here is a published contract surface. It
follows the pattern `ops/token-contract.md` and `ops/rendered-output-harness.md` set: every value is
marked as either a decision or an observation, and the two are never presented as the same kind of
fact (NFR-9). An observed value also carries the method that gathered it, because a number without a
method is a claim.

**Story ids are written hyphenated**, as `Story 1-12`, matching the keys in
`_bmad-output/implementation-artifacts/sprint-status.yaml`. `epics.md` writes the same ids dotted.
They are the same stories.

## The subsetting decisions, and the measured cost that forced one axis pin

**Observed 2026-08-25** on the Windows 11 development host, fontTools 4.60.1 through
`uv run --with "fonttools[woff]==4.60.1"`, latin subset (the Google Fonts `latin` unicode range),
layout features `kern,liga,calt,ccmp,locl,mark,mkmk,tnum`, woff2 flavour, hinting kept. Each figure
is the size of the woff2 that configuration produces.

| Variant | woff2 bytes |
|---|---|
| Bricolage, all axes as upstream ships them (`opsz` 12 to 96, `wdth` 75 to 100, `wght` 200 to 800) | 125,304 |
| Bricolage, `wght` 700 to 800, `wdth` 75 to 100, `opsz` 12 to 48 | 97,932 |
| Bricolage, same, `opsz` 12 to 24 | 83,264 |
| **Bricolage, same, `opsz` pinned at 24 (published)** | **58,992** |
| Bricolage, same, `opsz` pinned at 24, `--no-hinting` | 59,212 |
| Bricolage, same, `opsz` pinned at 24 and `wdth` pinned at 100 | 32,360 |
| **Geist, `wght` 300 to 600 (published)** | **24,124** |
| Geist, `wght` 300 to 600, `--no-hinting` | 24,084 |
| **Geist Mono, `wght` pinned at 400 (published)** | **11,284** |
| Geist Mono, `wght` pinned at 400, `--no-hinting` | 11,312 |

**The `opsz` pin.** **Decision.** Keeping the design's full `opsz` range puts the three-file total at
133,340 bytes against a budget of 120,000, and the budget is an acceptance criterion. Narrowing
`opsz` to 12 to 24 lands at 118,672, which passes by 1,328 bytes, or 1.1 percent. That is not margin,
it is a coin toss against the next value change, so `opsz` is pinned. The other two axes survive
because `DESIGN.md:458` names the width axis as the identity the design is carrying over from
Monument Extended, and the last row above is what pinning it would have been worth: 26,632 bytes for
the thing the type system exists to do.

**The pin value is 24**, the geometric midpoint of the 12 to 48 range `DESIGN.md:447` names as used,
chosen because optical-size perception is logarithmic rather than linear. **Decision.**

**Upstream's `opsz` floor is 12 and `DESIGN.md:447` says 10.** **Observed 2026-08-25**, by reading
`fvar` on the pinned source: `opsz` runs 12 to 96. The design names a value its own font cannot
produce. That is a wording defect in a planning artifact this story may not edit, and it is Pending
Operator action 1 rather than a change here.

**Hinting is kept, and it is not the 10 KB decision it is usually described as.** **Observed
2026-08-25**, from the table above: `--no-hinting` changes these three faces by +220, -40 and +28
bytes. All three upstream sources carry no TrueType instructions, so there is nothing for the flag to
strip and what small movement there is comes from adjacent tables. Keeping hinting is therefore free
here, and dropping it would have been a rendering-quality decision made for no gain.

**The unicode range and the feature list are pinned in `packages/fonts/sources.json`.**
**Decision.** The range is the Google Fonts `latin` subset, and the feature list keeps kerning,
standard and contextual ligatures, glyph composition, localised forms, mark attachment and tabular
figures. `tnum` is on the list because `DESIGN.md:490` requires `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`
on every count, plate mark and metric, and a subset that dropped `tnum` would make that rule a
no-op in seven repositories.

## The budget

UX-DR7 and `epics.md:1595` fix the number: three subsetted variable faces, **120 KB gzipped** total,
inside a 140 KB non-3D path. KB is 1000 bytes throughout, which is the reading the story's own
arithmetic uses. Taking KB as 1024 bytes would only widen the ceiling, so the narrower reading is the
safe one.

| Face | Bytes on disk | Bytes gzipped | Glyphs | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `bricolage-grotesque-latin.woff2` | 58,992 | 59,030 | 262 | **Observed 2026-08-25** |
| `geist-latin.woff2` | 24,124 | 24,152 | 264 | **Observed 2026-08-25** |
| `geist-mono-latin.woff2` | 11,284 | 11,307 | 309 | **Observed 2026-08-25** |
| **Total** | **94,400** | **94,489** | 835 | **Observed 2026-08-25** |
| Budget | 120,000 | 120,000 | n/a | **Decision.** UX-DR7 |
| Margin | 25,600 bytes, 21.3 percent | 25,511 bytes, 21.3 percent | n/a | **Derived** |

**Method.** Bytes on disk are `stat` on the committed file. Gzipped bytes are
`gzip.compress(data, compresslevel=9, mtime=0)` in `packages/fonts/subset.py`, with the timestamp
pinned so the figure is reproducible rather than moving with the clock.
`packages/fonts/__tests__/fonts-contract.test.ts` re-measures both from the committed binaries with
Node's `zlib` on every unit run, so the recorded figures cannot drift from the files they describe.

**Gzipping a woff2 makes it larger, and that is expected.** **Observed 2026-08-25.** woff2 is
Brotli-compressed already, so gzip adds a header and a few dozen bytes of framing rather than
removing anything. The gzipped figure is therefore the larger of the two here and it is the one the
requirement names, so both are asserted rather than one being assumed to follow from the other.

**One thing that nearly hid this.** **Observed 2026-08-25.** `subset.Options().flavor = "woff2"` is
read by fontTools' own command-line entry point, not by `Subsetter.subset`, so setting it there alone
writes an uncompressed TrueType file with a `.woff2` name. The first run of this generator produced
204,736 bytes across the three faces and refused on the budget. The flavour has to be set on the font
object that gets saved, and it now is, with a comment at the line saying why.

## The metric overrides

**Why the face is adjusted to the fallback and not the other way round.** **Decision.** With
`font-display: swap` the fallback paints first and the face replaces it. The thing that must not move
is the layout that the fallback already produced, so the fallback's metrics are the target.

Measured in one browser, at one pixel size, over one string, so both sides of every ratio come from
the same instrument:

```
sizeAdjust       = fallback.advance / face.advance
ascentOverride   = fallback.ascent  / (pixelSize * sizeAdjust)
descentOverride  = fallback.descent / (pixelSize * sizeAdjust)
lineGapOverride  = fallback.lineGap / (pixelSize * sizeAdjust)
```

The overrides are divided by the size adjustment because the browser applies them to the
size-adjusted em. `line-gap-override` is emitted only when the measured fallback carries a non-zero
line gap: emitting `0%` unconditionally would be a different claim, and omitting it when the fallback
has one would reintroduce the shift this whole mechanism exists to remove. All three fallbacks in the
pinned image do carry one, so all three rules carry the descriptor.

| Instrument | Value | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| Image | `mcr.microsoft.com/playwright:v1.62.1-noble` | **Decision.** The same image the rendered-output harness pins, deliberately: one image for every browser measurement in this repository |
| Index digest | `sha256:dcc5531e97840b9b5e794f2814476b21571c5124a3fca2267d73041f56e7580e` | **Observed 2026-08-25**, by `docker image inspect --format '{{index .RepoDigests 0}}'` |
| Chromium | 151.0.7922.34 | **Observed 2026-08-25**, from `browser.version()` inside the container |
| Pixel size | 100 | **Decision.** A round number, so a printed figure reads directly as a percentage of the em |
| Sample string | `Handgloves ABCxyz 0123` | **Decision.** Caps, ascenders, descenders, a space and digits, so the advance is not dominated by one glyph class |

**The measurements**, all **observed 2026-08-25** inside that image by
`packages/fonts/measure.mjs`. Ascent and descent are `fontBoundingBoxAscent` and
`fontBoundingBoxDescent` from a canvas `TextMetrics`; the line gap is what a `line-height: normal`
box holds beyond the two, because canvas exposes no line gap of its own.

| Bricolage Grotesque | `"Archivo", system-ui, sans-serif` | WenQuanYi Zen Hei | 1112.79 / 96 / 30 / 9 | 1206 / 93 / 27 |
| Geist | `ui-sans-serif, system-ui, sans-serif` | WenQuanYi Zen Hei | 1112.79 / 96 / 30 / 9 | 1169 / 101 / 30 |

and waits a frame first, and refuses rather than recording a blank.

**Why the measurement is committed rather than run at build time.** **Decision.**
`pnpm fonts:build` then stays deterministic arithmetic over two committed JSON files, which a CI
runner with no browser, no Python and no font toolchain reproduces byte for byte. That is what makes
the drift gate a real gate rather than a re-render. The cost is that `fallback-metrics.json` can go
stale, and the empirical check in `tests/e2e/contract-fonts.pw.ts` is what catches that: it measures
the actual swap in the actual browser and fails if the descriptors no longer hold the layout still.

## The swap check

**Why it is a geometry comparison and not a screenshot.** **Decision.** `epics.md:1610-1611` asks for
"a baseline comparison across the swap". A pixel comparison across a font swap fails by construction:
different outlines are different pixels, and that is the intended change. What must not move is the
layout. So `tests/e2e/contract-fonts.pw.ts` measures the same page twice, once with every woff2
request aborted and once with them allowed, which is exactly what a visitor experiences under
`font-display: swap`. The committed screenshot baseline is a different instrument, it belongs to
`/work`, and nothing in this story touches it.

The page it measures is served by a `node:http` server on an ephemeral port from a scratch tree with
`contracts/` copied to `assets/static/vendor/third-party/design/cuatro-contracts/`, five directories
deep under the fixed vendored folder name AD-14 requires. A `url()` that assumed a document root 404s
there instead of resolving by luck.

**The tolerances.** Four numbers rather than one, because the four kinds of row measure different
things and one number would have to be the loosest of them. Every figure below is **observed
2026-08-25** in the pinned container.

| Row | Tolerance | Worst observed | Margin below | Smallest breach the stripped-overrides probe produces | Margin above |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Height, every block | **1%** | 0.00% | unbounded | 2.96% | 2.96 times |
| Width at 100px, the pixel size the descriptors were measured at | **1%** | 0.25% | 4.0 times | 5.05% | 5.05 times |
| Width at 16px, the same pinned string | **3%** | 1.66% | 1.8 times | 7.84% | 2.61 times |
| Width of the prose block | **0.5%** | 0.00% | unbounded | n/a | n/a |

**Why the 16px width row is looser than the 100px one.** **Decision.** `size-adjust` is one scalar,
and at 16px each glyph's advance is rounded at a size-adjusted em, so the rounding accumulates over a
22 character string. 1.66 percent of 178 pixels is under 3 pixels. Both sides of the comparison are
deterministic in the pinned image, so 1.66 percent is a stable figure rather than noise to be
absorbed, which is why the tolerance is 3 percent and not 5.

**What the prose block is for.** **Decision.** Its width is its container's by construction, so that
row is an invariant rather than a measurement. It earns its place through its height, which is what
re-wrapping moves, and its text is prose the descriptors were never fitted to.

**What the swap check does not cover.** **Decision, stated because a passing gate invites more trust
than it has earned.** `size-adjust` is fitted to one string. A block of arbitrary text set in the
face will differ in width from the same block set in the fallback by whatever the two faces' relative
glyph widths differ by, and no single scalar can fix that. Claiming otherwise would be claiming the
two faces are metric-compatible, which they are not and are not meant to be. What the descriptors do
guarantee, and what this gate measures, is that the line box height does not move and that text
fitted to the measured string keeps its width.

## How the faces and the file are regenerated

Three commands, and only the third runs in CI.

```
corepack pnpm fonts:prepare    # subsets the three faces from the pinned sources
corepack pnpm fonts:measure    # measures the fallbacks in the pinned browser image
corepack pnpm fonts:build      # emits contracts/fonts.css from the two files above
```

**`fonts:prepare`** shells out to
`uv run --with "fonttools[woff]==4.60.1" python packages/fonts/subset.py`. The fontTools version is
pinned exactly, with no range: the woff2 bytes a subsetter emits are a function of its version, so a
floating pin would make the committed binaries and their recorded hashes disagree with a fresh run
for reasons nobody changed. `uv` is not on PATH on the authoring host, so `CUATRO_UV` is accepted as
a full path to it. Run twice from the same sources it produces byte-identical output, **observed
2026-08-25** by comparing sha256 across two consecutive runs.

**That determinism had to be bought.** **Observed 2026-08-25.** fontTools stamps `head.modified` with
the current time on every save unless told otherwise, so the first two runs of this script produced
different binaries, different hashes and byte counts 172 bytes apart. `recalcTimestamp=False` on
every open and every save is what fixes it, and there is a comment at that line saying so.

**`fonts:measure`** must run inside `mcr.microsoft.com/playwright:v1.62.1-noble` and nowhere else.
The command used on 2026-08-25, from the repository root on the Windows host:

```
docker run --rm --ipc=host ^
  -v C:/CuatroEcosystem/cuatro-portfolio:/w ^
  -v pw-node-modules:/w/node_modules ^
  -v pw-next:/w/.next ^
  -w /w -e CI=1 ^
  -e CUATRO_FONTS_IMAGE_DIGEST=sha256:dcc5531e97840b9b5e794f2814476b21571c5124a3fca2267d73041f56e7580e ^
```

The two named volumes matter for the same reason `ops/rendered-output-harness.md:166-173` gives:
`node_modules` and `.next` on the Windows host hold Windows binaries a Linux container cannot
execute. The digest is passed in rather than looked up inside the container, because a container has
no way to name the image it is running.

**`fonts:build`** is Node builtins over two committed JSON files plus the version in
`packages/tokens/package.json`. It needs no environment, no browser and no Python.

**When a face changes, four things move together** and a change to fewer than all four is a defect:
the pinned entry in `packages/fonts/sources.json`, the committed source under
`packages/fonts/sources/`, the regenerated binaries and `faces.json`, and the figures in this file.
`fallback-metrics.json` moves too whenever a face's own advance changes, because `size-adjust` is a
ratio of it.

## Why the generator carries no manifest

`packages/fonts` has no `package.json`, deliberately. **Decision.**

It needs no npm dependency: `build.mjs` is Node builtins, subsetting is `uv` and fontTools, and
`measure.mjs` borrows the root's `@playwright/test` through ordinary Node resolution. A manifest
there would make it a workspace importer, which changes the lockfile and obliges a new `COPY` line in
the Docker `deps` stage. That is the exact failure `docker/__tests__/deps-stage.test.ts` was written
for after Story 1-11's review predicted this story would trip it, and the consequence is a failed
deploy from `main`, the one place with no staging behind it.

**Verified rather than assumed**, **observed 2026-08-25**:
`corepack pnpm install --frozen-lockfile` with `packages/fonts` present reports "Lockfile is up to
date, resolution step is skipped" and two workspace projects, and `pnpm-lock.yaml`,
`pnpm-workspace.yaml` and `docker/Dockerfile` are byte-identical to `7545cd6`. A directory under
`packages/` with no `package.json` is not a workspace package, which is what
`docker/__tests__/deps-stage.test.ts:66-77` already encodes.

## Stated limits

Naming these is the point of the section: a contract that ships is easily mistaken for a contract
that covers everything.

| Limit | Why it stands | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| The overrides are tuned against a Linux container's fallback, not against Segoe UI or SF Pro | All three `--f-*` stacks resolve to WenQuanYi Zen Hei or WenQuanYi Zen Hei Mono in the pinned image. A Windows or macOS visitor gets a different first fallback with different metrics, so the swap on their machine is held still less exactly than the numbers above suggest. Measuring per platform would need three browsers on three operating systems in CI, which this estate does not have and is not going to buy | **Observed 2026-08-25**, from `CSS.getPlatformFontsForNode` |
| Two of the three stacks resolve to the same fallback face in that image | `"Archivo", system-ui, sans-serif` and `ui-sans-serif, system-ui, sans-serif` both land on WenQuanYi Zen Hei, so the display and body faces are tuned against one set of metrics rather than two. That is what the image has, not a shortcut | **Observed 2026-08-25** |
| The faces are latin only, and the `unicode-range` says so | Any codepoint outside the published range falls back silently, exactly as it would with no face at all. Non-latin content in a Satellite is out of scope for this contract and is not a defect in it | **Decision.** UX-DR7 |
| Nothing adopts the contract yet | The Hub still declares its own `@font-face` block in `app/scss/_fonts.scss` and still serves `public/fonts/`. Publishing is not adopting: Story 1.17 adds the files to the Hub's stylesheet graph, Story 1.18 aliases onto them, and Story 2.20 retires the old binaries | **Decision.** Story 1-12 scope |
| The subsetting step has no executing check anywhere | The unit gate asserts the committed binaries against the hashes `faces.json` records, and the drift gate rebuilds the stylesheet. Neither runs fontTools, so `subset.py`'s own three refusals are covered by this record and by one real observation, not by a test | **Decision**, recorded rather than fixed |
| The `fonts-contract` job has never run on a GitHub runner | Everything recorded about it was observed by running its commands locally against the committed tree | **Observed 2026-08-25.** Pending Operator action 2 |
| The `@font-face` rules declare `font-style: normal` only | No italic face is published, for any family. `DESIGN.md:502` forbids synthesised italic and italic headings, so a consumer that sets `font-style: italic` gets a synthesised slant rather than a designed one. Publishing three more binaries to close that would cost roughly the whole remaining budget | **Decision** |
