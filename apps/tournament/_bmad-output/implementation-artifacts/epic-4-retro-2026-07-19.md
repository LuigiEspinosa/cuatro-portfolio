## What Went Well (keep doing this)

1. **Review-by-execution caught real correctness bugs, repeatedly.** 4.3's `40P01` deadlock (Winners-Final vs Losers-Final racing on the Grand Final) and the `match_place_competitor` double-route (a decided winner flipped `X → Y`, a winner NULLed to success) were *reproduced against the shipped functions*, not argued. 4.7's forfeit-denylist gap was flagged **independently by all three layers** (Blind=CRITICAL, Edge=HIGH, Auditor=HIGH). This is the strongest quality mechanism in the build.
2. **The patch curve shows the team learning in real time** — 4.1 (11 patch) · 4.2 (11) · 4.3 (16) → 4.4 (2) → … → 4.9 (0 correctness defects). The "mutation-test your own suite" lesson moved upstream and stuck.
3. **"One named decision per story, resolved before dev" held all ten stories** — carried straight from Epic 2/3.
4. **The Epic 3 → 4 hand-off list landed in full** — both deferred FKs (`demo_match_fk`, `stat_row_match_fk`, migration 0010), the roster-lock TOCTOU (0011 `roster_entry_lock` trigger, which *blocks* a concurrent write rather than merely narrowing the window), and 4.6's absorbed 3.7 scope.
5. **Splitting 4.6 instead of shipping a monster** — 4.6a/4.6b — and 4.9 explicitly invoked that lesson (DECISION E) to *defer* the undo tools rather than re-create the size problem.
6. **Typed-refusal discipline scaled to a much harder epic** — RPCs RETURN `{ok:false}` for every guard and RAISE only for the impossible; canonical whole-bracket lock ordering; post-commit-only emits; the DB-only ↔ worker-only slice boundary (a green `git diff` as a proof) held.

## Key Insights / Breakthroughs

- **"The suite proves materially less than it claims."** Three consecutive reviews (4.1 → 4.2 → 4.3) found **green** pgTAP suites blind to the actual invariant. In 4.3, deleting AC2's result-state gate left **79/79 green** while the bracket double-routed a `pending` match — the exact AD-8 failure. The irony: 4.3 mutation-tested AC1's predicate, found *that* hole, fixed it, and shipped the identical hole on AC2. The cure was to move **"mutation-test your own suite BEFORE review"** upstream as a pre-review practice — visible from 4.4 onward (its Auditor was the first to verify the story's load-bearing claims were TRUE).
- **A whole class of migration hazard that `supabase db reset` structurally cannot catch.** 4.1's FKs could not sit on the external-id columns (would `23503` every ingest at runtime); 4.3's immediate-validated CHECK over eight un-backfilled columns cannot apply to a non-empty `match` table. Because `db reset` starts from an empty schema, the local gate is *blind* to runtime/upgrade failures — so each is recorded in its migration header for first deploy. This is a live risk for Epic 7.
- **"A binding existing does not mean it is valid" (4.6a → 4.6b).** 4.6a bound `(match, demo)` on the admin's say-so and verified nothing about their relationship; the review homed `wrong_demo` + `demo_mismatch` guards to 4.6b by name. A trust boundary needs an explicit guard, not an assumption.
- **AD-20 makes the leaderboard recompute a no-op by design.** 4.6b's Aprobar "recompute leaderboards" step is deliberately empty — the `status='approved'` flip *is* the recompute once Story 5.5 builds the leaderboard as a view over approved `stat_row`s. Clean hand-off, no `approve_match` revision needed — provided 5.5 keeps that contract.

## What Was Hard (systems-level, no blame)

- **The blind-suite arc (4.1–4.3)** — the epic's defining discomfort; addressed by making mutation-testing a pre-review habit rather than a review-time catch.
- **Migration-apply / upgrade hazards invisible to `db reset`** — a recurring class, now header-recorded, that will only bite at first hosted deploy (Epic 7).
- **deferred-work.md is carrying real weight.** It works — items get homed by name and closed — but a few load-bearing gaps are accruing: **no path undoes a committed `forfeit`** (4.7 defer + 4.9 DECISION E); the **`ceremony_locked` guard owed to Story 6.2** (AD-15) on approve/rollback/manual-score; `advance_match`'s bare `P0001`s (DECISION F); CSRF (Epic 7); the competitor-column seam.
- **The "widest ripple" refactor persisted** — most command RPCs copied a prior RPC's body forward (`mark_walkover` → `approve_match` → `manual_resolve_match` → `rollback_match`), a clean template each time but a standing signature-blast-radius tax.
