## Story

As an admin,
I want to roll back an Approved match and have dependent advances reverted first,
so that unpublishing a result never leaves the bracket in an inconsistent state.

## Acceptance Criteria

Verbatim from [epics.md#Story 4.7 (lines 763-783)]. Traces: **FR-14 (rollback) · AD-8**.

**AC1 — the transitive-rollback rule (AD-8, FR-14).**
**Given** the transitive-rollback rule, **When** an admin rolls back an Approved match, **Then** dependent advances are reverted transitively *before* the match's stats are unpublished.

**AC2 — idempotency (AD-8).**
**Given** idempotency, **When** a rollback is applied, **Then** it is server-gated, re-verifies `is_admin()`, and is idempotent.

**AC3 — the dual-ownership split.**
**Given** the dual-ownership split, **When** this story rolls back, **Then** re-parsing a retained demo is owned by Story 3.6.

### AC1's SECOND, LOAD-BEARING CLAUSE — the AD-8 flag, missing from the epic AC but the whole point

The epic AC1 says *"dependent advances are reverted transitively."* It does **not** state the constraint that makes the word "transitively" safe, but **AD-8 (`ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md:118`) does**, verbatim: *"Rollback reverts dependent advances transitively before unpublishing, **and flags — never auto-reverts — a downstream match already approved on its own demo**."* AD-8's *Prevents* names the exact hazard by name: *"a rollback silently un-publishing unrelated matches."* **Treat this as part of AC1.** A rollback that cascade-destroys a downstream match which was itself legitimately played and Approved is the single worst thing this story can do, and it is the reason a naive "just un-advance everything" implementation is wrong.

### FR-14's leaderboard + return-state clauses (real, and cheaper than they look)

FR-14 (`prd.md:255`): *"Rolling back an Approved Match **removes its contribution from leaderboards** and **reverts dependent Bracket advancement**, **returning it to Pending or unparsed**."* Two of those three are nearly free here and one is a DECISION:
- **"removes its contribution from leaderboards"** — a **NO-OP you must not build**, exactly as it was for Aprobar (4.6b DECISION E, AD-20). The leaderboard is *"one SQL view/RPC over `status='approved'` rows"* (`SPINE:178`); flipping the stat rows back to `status='pending'` (STEP below) **is** the removal. There is nothing to recompute. Story 5.5 still owns the view.
- **"returning it to Pending or unparsed"** — the match returns to **`pending`** (demo still bound, re-approvable). See **DECISION A** — this is the headline decision and it is already pinned by five shipped migrations.

## THE ONE-PARAGRAPH HEADLINE

**`rollback_match` is `approve_match` run backwards, and the only genuinely new code is the reverse of the advance cascade — which must refuse, not cascade, when it meets a match that stands on its own demo.** `approve_match` (0017) took a `pending`, demo-bound match and, in one transaction, flipped its stat rows to `approved`, wrote the demo-derived score + `state='resolved'`, called `advance_match` (which seated the winner downstream, dropped the loser to Losers, and walked any bye cascade), posted a `timeline_feed` entry, wrote an `approve` audit row, and emitted one `match.approved`. **Your job is to undo each of those, in reverse, atomically** — but step 3 (the advance) does not reverse by symmetry. When you approved match M, `advance_match` seated M's winner into a downstream slot; that slot may since have been **filled from the other side and played and Approved on its own demo**. AD-8 forbids you to un-publish *that* match as a side effect — you must **flag it and refuse the whole rollback** (leaf-first: the admin rolls the downstream one back first). So the reverse cascade is a **two-pass, read-only-then-apply** walk exactly like `advance_match`'s (mirror its worklist so the routing lives in **one** place, D1) whose planning pass detects any downstream `resolved`/`manual_resolved`/`pending`/`live` match and RETURNS `{ok:false, reason:'downstream_active'}` having written nothing. Only when every dependent seat is still purely structural (`declared`/`bye`/`void`) do you un-seat them (the conditional inverse of `match_place_competitor` — un-seat **only** the entry you placed, which is what makes AC2's idempotency fall out for free), then unpublish M back to `pending`, write one `rollback` audit row, and emit once. **The state `rolled_back` already exists in the closed set and the whole codebase has been waiting for `resolved → pending` — but the two contradict each other in migration comments; DECISION A resolves it.**

### The mechanism, precisely — every ordering constraint below is load-bearing

1. **The canonical lock, UP FRONT, before the first write.** Byte-identical to `approve_match` (`0017:257-271`) and `advance_match` (`0014:382-400`): unlocked peek for `tournament_id`, then `select 1 from match where tournament_id = v_tid order by m.id for update`. Your reverse cascade reads and writes many rows of the bracket; take every lock in `id` order up front or reproduce the 4.3 `40P01` deadlock.
2. **PLAN the whole revert read-only, THEN apply — the two-pass invariant (AC1 "before unpublishing").** Pass 1 walks the dependent advances and **detects the AD-8 flag**; if it fires, RETURN `{ok:false}` having written nothing. Only after pass 1 proves the revert is clean do you (pass 2) un-seat, then unpublish M. This is what makes "dependent advances are reverted transitively *before* the match's stats are unpublished" literally true, and it is `advance_match`'s exact structure.
3. **Un-publish M in the reverse order of `approve_match`.** approve did: stat rows → `approved`; score+`state='resolved'`; advance; feed; audit; emit. You undo: revert the advance (pass 2 un-seats) **first**; then `match` score/state back to `pending`; then stat rows back to `pending`; then (clear `final_match_id` if this was the deciding row); then the audit row; then one emit. The *"before unpublishing"* clause pins the advance-revert ahead of the stat-status flip.
4. **One emit, after commit, by construction.** Exactly as `approve_match` (`0017:424-442`) and `advance_match` (`0014:632-646`): a single `realtime.send(...)` writes a row in *your* transaction that Realtime ships only on commit — the emit cannot outrun its own commit. See DECISION G for the event name.
